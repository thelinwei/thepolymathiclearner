易 Yi · 07 · Field note · October 2026 · After the letters

The hexagramas field

and the Yi as song.

The archive went out to readers, and some wrote back. Their questions pointed in one direction: transitions, propensity and meaning, held as part of the dynamics rather than added afterwards. This note records where that led — to sixty-four fields in place of sixty-four points, to the hexagrams heard as keys in a bounded song, and to a search for a score that history has already written.

00 · Return

The work went out, and came back changed.

For two weeks in October 2026 the archive was sent to scholars, designers and mathematician-artists, one letter at a time. Some letters were answered, and a few of the answers were full readings of the work.

The replies that went deepest asked the same things from different directions: make the transitions as explicit as the states; let tendency belong to the situation rather than arrive as an outside force; treat the text's meaning as part of the dynamics; give time its own axis. Those readers will be thanked here by name, with their permission, in a later edition of this note.

What follows does not answer the feedback head on. It moves sideways, through Walsh functions and pitch-pipes and Spring and Autumn divination records, and then, by a route that was not planned, arrives back at exactly those questions. The archive has grown this way from the start: a divergence that seems to open a new realm, and a return that finds the realm was adjacent all along. The return is where the learning is kept.

言不尽意
“Words do not exhaust meaning.” The model is a 象 xiang, an image, in its own right. It can disclose; it is never the whole.《系辞上》 · a framing for the whole project
01 · From vertex to field

The sixty-four were becoming points.

Part One began with a continuum: each hexagram a region of a six-dimensional space, with an interior, margins and a velocity. The search for one beautiful body — lantern, loom — drew the sixty-four back into beads on a thread. A reader looking at them sees beads.

Read together, those questions suggest the hexagram is closer to a field than a vertex. The word carries more than one meaning, and each meaning builds a different model. Four are set side by side below, without yet choosing among them; the exploration is still open, and the delineations can wait.

夫卦者，时也；
爻者，适时之变者也。
“A hexagram is a time. The lines are the changes that suit that time.”王弼 Wang Bi · 《周易略例·明卦适变通爻》 · translation by the author

Wang Bi's sentence from the third century holds that whole brief in thirteen characters. The hexagram is a situation that lasts and has its own character of movement — a season more than a coordinate. On this reading the shift that matters runs from noun to verb, from position to situation. Continuity alone does not achieve it: a smooth field can still be a map of places, and Newtonian physics is full of smooth fields. What the replies ask for is a field of tendency.

The threshold still matters. Much of the tradition's force lies in the qualitative turn — one line changes and the whole situation becomes another. A model that melts every boundary into a gradient loses the moment of 变. The working position: a continuum underneath, thresholds that are real, and hexagrams as named times, each with its own tendency.
02 · Sixty-four fields

Each hexagram is also a way of reading all the others.

There are exactly sixty-four Walsh functions on six binary places, and each is named by a six-line pattern. Read through them, every hexagram becomes a field: a +1 or −1 laid over the whole space of situations.

The rule is short. Hexagram k asks every situation one question: among the lines where k is yang, is the count of yang lines in this situation even or odd? Even gives +1, odd gives −1. The sixty-four fields are mutually orthogonal and together form a complete basis, so any quality that can be attached to situations — auspiciousness in the text, a semantic theme, a reading of a neighbourhood — can be written exactly, and in one way only, as a weighted mixture of the sixty-four.

A situation then has a spectrum, and change is weight moving across it. Select a hexagram on the board to see the field it casts. The rows are upper trigrams, the columns lower trigrams, both in the 先天 xiantian order.

Read through time instead of space, the same object becomes rhythm. Each line is a clock, a square wave, and the six clocks halve in period from the bottom line to the top — the timescale ladder of Part Two and the 2, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 cascade the loom revealed. A hexagram selects the clocks of its yang lines and multiplies them: it is the interference pattern of the clocks it listens to.

Line clocks over one bar of 64 beats
You are hearing chime-bells modelled on the 曾侯乙编钟 (Marquis Yi of Zeng, 433 BCE), struck singly or as a group. The voice is taken from recorded strikes: small excavated bronze bells, and the great lower bells of a Zeng replica struck with poles. A bright clang, then a hum that blooms after the strike and rings on, for seconds in a small bell and twenty in a great one. Let the low bells ring.
The listening is an illustration: each yang line of the selected hexagram is a chime-bell on 宫 商 角 徵 羽, after the 曾侯乙编钟, struck as its clock rises; a deeper bell marks each turn of the product wave. Nothing measured is being played.
01 · computed

应 is three fields

The classical correspondence between lines 1–4, 2–5 and 3–6 is exactly the field of 震为雷, 坎为水 and 艮为山 — the three doubled trigrams with a single yang line. Each is +1 where the pair agrees and −1 where they answer one another, 有应. The model's 土 pairing, the commentary's 应 and these three hexagrams are one object.

02 · computed

A moving line reorganises half

When one line of a situation turns, every field that listens to that line flips sign — exactly thirty-two of sixty-four — and the other thirty-two are untouched. A 变爻 is a precise reorganisation of the whole spectrum.

03 · computed

错卦 sorts by parity

Inverting every line of a situation flips exactly the fields with an odd number of yang lines and leaves the even ones. 综卦, turning the hexagram over, permutes the fields among themselves.

04 · computed

The loom's thread returns

Ordering the Walsh functions from slowest to fastest is the reflected Gray code, bit-reversed — the same thread that wove the loom. The two may be one structure seen from two sides. Shao Yong's 先天 order, binary counting, is the basis's natural index.

Where it stops. The correspondence is guaranteed by arithmetic, since sixty-four is two to the sixth; it is an option for the model, not evidence about the Yi. In this encoding 坤 is the constant field, the empty product, a ground shared by every situation — apt to some readers (坤厚载物, the earth that carries all things) and an objection to others, for whom yin is never absence. Binary readings of the Yi run from Leibniz to Boolean algebra; Walsh functions have been noticed once before, as matrix algebra in an argument about the genetic code. A reading of each hexagram as a field over situations, tested against the text, was not found in the literature checked.

03 · The song

Keys and tones, with durations, in a bounded time.

A hexagram is less a fixed rhythm than a key or a tone, held for a while. Moved through in a fitting arrangement and sequence, the tones make a song that is meaningful and beautiful as a whole. Beneath it runs 潮起潮落, the rise and fall of tides: no single note can be heard alone, and nothing stops changing.

The practice this asks for has three parts: to anticipate the movement, to accept the entirety of the song, and from there to appreciate its structure, beauty and meaning. A song here means a bounded stretch of time and experience — a life, an office, a dynasty, a season.

The image fits the model better than the language of force did. A key does not dictate which note sounds next; it colours every note, and makes some tensions want to resolve. That pull is a musical 势, a propensity carried by the harmony itself. A situation is a chord, several of the sixty-four sounding with different weights, named for its dominant voice. A moving line is a modulation, the turn through a pivot into another key. And 应 is consonance between the lower and upper voices, already named in the 文言: 同声相应，同气相求 — like sounds answer each other, like breaths seek each other.

Vertical · the chord

Six lines sounding at once.

The dynamical model has so far listened this way: six simultaneous variables, related as voices through 应 and 比. The fields of section 02 are this hearing made exact.

Horizontal · the melody

Six bars, from 初 to 上.

The line statements read the same six positions as a passage through time — a beginning, a rise, a peak, an excess. Each hexagram is a short song in its own key, and the 序卦 strings the sixty-four into a longer one.

初 · bar 1潜龙勿用The dragon lies hidden. Do not act.
二 · bar 2见龙在田The dragon appears in the field.
三 · bar 3君子终日乾乾All day the noble one strives, and at dusk stays watchful.
四 · bar 4或跃在渊Leaping, or still in the deep.
五 · bar 5飞龙在天The dragon flies in the heavens.
上 · bar 6亢龙有悔The dragon overreaches; there is regret.

乾 read horizontally · six bars of one bounded passage, rising to the fifth and overreaching at the sixth · translations by the author

潮起潮落 · the twelve 消息卦 xiaoxi gua, one line at a time: yang returns at the base in 复, fills to 乾, yin enters at the base in 姤, empties to 坤, and the tide turns again. The Han scholars heard this cycle as the year and as the twelve pitch-pipes together.

The tradition already names the practice. Anticipation is 知几 zhiji: 几者，动之微，吉之先见者也 — the incipient stir of movement, in which what is coming first shows itself. A trained listener feels where a phrase is going before it arrives, not as a predicted note but as the pull of the key. Acceptance of the whole is 乐天知命，故不忧. And the 周易 itself is a bounded song that ends unresolved: its last two hexagrams are 既济, already across, and then 未济, not yet across — a closing chord that leans into the next song.

Fittingness, held lightly. If a song is beautiful in one arrangement only, the Yi becomes a score to obey. 中 and 时 point instead to what suits this moment in this song — closer to a musician improvising inside a form than to a correct sequence. The agency stays with the person in the song, where 感应 would want it.
The open edge: octaves and fifths. Harmony built from Walsh functions knows only doubling; everything in a binary basis is an octave of something. The Chinese pitch system is built on 三分损益, the ratio 3 : 2 that makes the fifth. A purely binary Yi gives rhythm and octave structure, but no fifth. If the song is to have harmony, a three must enter from somewhere — the four values 6, 7, 8, 9 of the milfoil count, the three lines of the trigram and 三才 sancai, or a ratio inside the dynamics. Which, if any, is not yet known.
04 · Looking for a score

History kept performances, and composed scores.

If the sixty-four can sound together, a model could learn to hear real sequences of them. Records of hexagrams cast in time do survive. They come in two kinds, and the difference decides what each can teach.

The richest use sets a composed score against a performance: Shao Yong's dynastic score against documented history, or the 卦气 calendar against centuries of weather. Where the two disagree, the music would be saying something.

The trap, named in advance. Transcribing a dynasty into hexagrams ourselves would be composing, not finding — the same error as assigning measured timescales to lines and then claiming the ordering as a result. The first listening exercises therefore use sequences the tradition itself recorded.
05 · Ledger

What this note establishes, and what it opens.

The habit of the sequence holds: claims are sorted by what supports them, and the open edges are left open.

Established
  • Each hexagram defines a Walsh field over all sixty-four situations; the sixty-four fields form a complete orthogonal basis.
  • 应 at 1–4, 2–5, 3–6 equals the fields of 震, 坎 and 艮 doubled.
  • A moving line flips exactly 32 of 64 fields; 错卦 flips exactly the odd ones.
  • Sequency order is the reflected Gray code, bit-reversed. All four checked by computation, 14 October 2026.
Provisional
  • The hexagram as field — territory, propensity, resonance, spectrum — as the reading those questions point toward.
  • The Yi heard as song: keys and tones with durations, chord and melody, modulation at the moving line.
  • The 左传 records as a corpus of transitions.
Open
  • Where a three enters, so the song can have fifths.
  • Whether the 序卦 has a grammar the fields can hear.
  • Whether 坤 as the constant field reads as ground or as absence, and to whom.
  • How to keep thresholds real inside a field of tendency.
Next
  • The 左传 casts as phrases: which fields flip, how many lines move, consonant or dissonant 应.
  • 包山 as one person's song, heard from first cast to last.
  • A Walsh spectrum of the 卦辞 and 爻辞 using a segmented edition of the text: where does 吉 and 凶 collect?
  • The 皇极经世 as the grand composed score, kept for later.
