Consonanceand couplets

The sixty-four, heard as chords on the bells.

Struck together, the six bells of a hexagram make a chord. Some chords sit open and calm, some pull hard against themselves. This note listens to all sixty-four that way, on the bells of 曾侯乙. They fall into nine harmonies. The received sequence of the 周易 turns out to be written in couplets, each pair stating one harmony twice. And the correspondence between lines, 应, sounds as a pull. Every finding is then put through the other tunings, to see which belong to the Yi and which belonged only to the tuning.

00 · Two hearings

Harmony is heard upright and in motion.

Vertically, a chord is consonant or tense in itself. Horizontally, a sequence of chords moves: tension rises, resolves, or is restated. A cycle of hexagrams can be heard both ways.

Three habits keep the listening honest. A pattern counts only if the real ordering does something that shuffled orderings rarely do. A finding counts as the Yi's only if it survives a change of tuning; what vanishes when the tuning changes belonged to the tuning. And the hand-made scale of consonance used here, which rates fifths and thirds as open and semitones and tritones as tense, is one ear among many, so results that depend on its exact weights are marked as fragile.

The sound and the default tuning are the chime-bells of 曾侯乙. Each line is a bell: yang is struck at the face, on one of the six yang pitches of Zheng Xuan's 爻辰, and yin at the side, a minor third higher. The Han pipe tuning, in which yin sits a fifth from yang, can be switched on for comparison.

01 · 六律 and 六吕

Pure yang and pure yin have no fifth.

Laid on a clock of twelve semitones, the six yang pipes fall on every other hour: a whole-tone scale. The six yin tones, struck at the bells' sides, fill the hours between: the other whole-tone scale. 乾 sounds one, 坤 the other.

A whole-tone scale has no fifth and no fourth, no centre to lean toward, and the music built from it famously floats. So the purest hexagrams are the least anchored. Harmony in the ordinary sense, fifths and thirds, appears only when the two scales mix, that is, when a hexagram holds both yin and yang. Choose any hexagram on the board: its six bells light on the clock, the even hours yang, the odd hours yin. The board is coloured by harmony, for the next section.

02 · Nine harmonies

Sixty-four chords, nine harmonies.

Treat two chords as one harmony when one is the other moved up or down, or turned upside down. On the bells, as with the Han pipes, the sixty-four hexagrams then fall into exactly nine harmonies, and several of the groups are ones the tradition already named.

The comparison follows pitch-class set theory, the method twentieth-century music theory built for exactly this question: when are two chords the same shape? The groups below are ordered from most open to most tense on the hand-made scale. The ordering is fragile; the grouping is not, because it does not depend on any weights.

03 · Couplets

The received sequence is written in couplets.

The 周易 orders its sixty-four as thirty-two pairs: each second hexagram is the first turned upside down, or, where that changes nothing, every line inverted. Heard on the bells, every pair carries exactly the same intervals. It is one harmony stated twice.

In twenty-six of the thirty-two pairs the second chord is literally the first moved or mirrored. The other six are twins: different chords built from identical intervals, the relation music theory calls Z-related. Those six are marked in gold. This is not a hidden code. It follows from the pairing rule of the 序卦 meeting the symmetry of the tuning. But it means that, as music, the received order proceeds in couplets, like the paired lines of 对仗 in verse, each harmony answered by its own reflection before the sequence moves on.

Select any pair to hear it as a couplet on the 曾侯乙 bells.
04 · 应 as pull

Correspondence sounds as attraction.

Lines one and four, two and five, three and six correspond. When the pair holds one yin and one yang, the tradition calls it 有应, answered. When both are the same, 无应, unanswered, sometimes 敌应, an enemy correspondence.

On the bells, an answered pair sits a minor third apart, the same distance as a bell's own two tones: close, consonant, a quiet answer. An unanswered pair sits a tritone apart, the interval that is unstable and has no direction to resolve in. The enemy correspondence sounds like what it is called. With the Han pipe tuning the answer tightens to a semitone, the leading tone that leans hardest into its neighbour, and 应 sounds as attraction under tension.

The tritone of 无应 holds in every tuning tested, because it comes from the 爻辰 assignment itself: the yang pitches of lines i and i+3 always stand six steps apart. The interval of 有应 depends on the tuning: a minor third on the bells, a whole tone with their major third, a semitone with the pipes.

05 · The tuning test

The pattern in the table has a single cause. The structure holds whenever the yin tone sits an odd number of semitones from the yang: a fifth is seven, a minor third three. Then yang and yin live in the two complementary whole-tone scales, and every hexagram is a meeting of two worlds. When the distance is even, a major third or an octave, yin falls back inside yang's own scale, and most of the structure collapses.

Read musically, the Yi's harmony needs yin to be other: a different scale, not yang moved along its own. The bells of 曾侯乙 sound both thirds, minor and major, and only the minor kind keeps the structure.
06 · Still wondering

Threads left open, on purpose.

Wondering

Couplets and 对仗

Chinese verse pairs its lines by reflection, and the 序卦 pairs its hexagrams the same way. Is the couplet a shared grammar of the culture, heard here in bronze?

Wondering

Groups the tradition named

The six doubled trigrams form one harmony; the hexagrams with a single yang or a single yin line form another; 既济 and 未济 stand alone. Why do pitch shapes and traditional families coincide?

Wondering

Between couplets

Inside a couplet the harmony holds. Between couplets it moves. The motion of the 序卦 may live entirely in those thirty-one joins.

Fragile

The ear's weights

Which harmony is "most open" depends on how the intervals are rated. The groupings do not. Another listener's scale may reorder the nine.
