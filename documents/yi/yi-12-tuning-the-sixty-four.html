Tuningthe sixty-four

Which number should a line be? A laboratory of assignments, for the ear to decide.

Every chapter of the listening so far heard a hexagram one way: each line a bell, rising by whole tones after 郑玄's 爻辰, yang struck at the face and yin at the side. That assignment has a lineage, and it is one choice among many. This note lays the others out side by side. A hexagram can be one note, two, or six. Its lines can weigh as binary digits, as places in a received order, as harmonics, as steps along the chain of fifths. Each assignment is played through eight received orderings, and each is compared with the same hexagrams shuffled. Where an ordering sounds more resonant than its own shuffles, the resonance comes from the meeting of the order and the assignment. That is the quality this note listens for.

00 · The question

An assignment is a hypothesis you can hear.

To sound a hexagram, something about it has to become a frequency. Whatever is chosen, its line positions, its place in an order, its count of yang, decides which relations between hexagrams the ear can follow.

Pitch is heard on a logarithmic ruler. Equal steps to the ear are equal ratios of frequency: an octave doubles, a semitone multiplies by the twelfth root of two. So asking for a chromatic assignment and asking for a logarithmic one are the same request. Each line is given a number of steps, and the steps are measured in ratios. The harmonic series counts the other way, in equal additions of frequency, and its steps narrow as they rise. Both rulers belong to the bronze: the chromatic one is how the Zeng bells were named and tuned, and the harmonic one is how every vibrating body sounds.

Any sequence can be made pleasant by snapping its notes to a scale, and that pleasantness says nothing about the sequence. This laboratory offers the snap only as a comparison. The quality it looks for is different: an assignment under which a received ordering, the 序卦, the 卦气 year, 京房's palaces, a man's last casts, sounds more resonant than the same hexagrams in random order. Resonance of that kind is found rather than imposed. It belongs to the relation between the ordering and the way of hearing it.

The measure of resonance is deliberately simple. For each step of an ordering, every pair of pitches sounding across the two neighbouring hexagrams is rated on the scale of consonance used in note 10: unisons and octaves fully open, fifths and fourths nearly so, thirds and sixths warm, whole tones mild, semitones and tritones tense. The average is the ordering's resonance under that assignment. Then the ordering is shuffled three hundred times and the same average taken, and the real ordering is placed among its shuffles as a percentile. A percentile near 100 means the received order finds the consonant joins far more often than chance. The number points the ear somewhere; the ear decides.

01 · Three ways

One voice, two voices, six.

A hexagram can be read whole, as a pair of trigrams, or line by line. Each reading gives a different instrument.

02 · Two rulers and a spiral

Sixty-four fits the bronze.

Laid on the chromatic ruler, the sixty-four hexagrams span sixty-three semitones. The Zeng bells span sixty-two, from C2 to D7. Laid on the harmonic ruler, they are the first sixty-four harmonics of a single tone, exactly six octaves.

Each mark is one hexagram, valued with the bottom line finest (坤 = 0, 乾 = 63) and placed on a logarithmic axis of pitch from C1 to C8. Chromatic: evenly spaced. Harmonic: crowded at the top, spacious below. Fifths: sixty-four steps of a pure fifth, folded into two octaves, gather near the twelve notes and slowly drift from them.
三分所生，益之一分以上生；
三分所生，去其一分以下生。
Of what is made by thirds: add a third, and the pipe generates downward; take away a third, and it generates upward.《吕氏春秋 · 音律》 · the rule of 三分损益

The third ruler is the chain of fifths that 三分损益 builds. Twelve steps of a pure fifth come back almost, but not quite, to the starting note, missing by about a quarter of a semitone. 京房 in the Western Han carried the chain past twelve to sixty pitches, the 六十律, so that the small remainders could themselves be named and used. Sixty-four hexagrams on that chain sit close to his sixty, and the same 京房 is the name attached to the 卦气 calendar and the eight palaces that appear below as orderings. The fit is suggestive and nothing more; this note uses the chain as one ruler among three.

03 · The catalogue

Every assignment, and what it lets you hear.

One voice is built from two choices: how the lines weigh, and which ruler turns the weight into pitch. Two voices and six voices are built line by line. Each card opens the laboratory on that assignment.

05 · The survey

Every assignment through every ordering.

Each cell places a received ordering among three hundred shuffles of itself, under one assignment. Gold cells are orderings that beat at least 95 of every 100 shuffles. Tap a cell to load it in the laboratory and hear it.

Two cautions travel with the table. Some assignments make an ordering resonant by construction: weighting lines by their place in the 序卦 and then playing the 序卦 gives a rising chromatic scale, every step a semitone. Those rows are kept because the construction is itself worth hearing, and their percentiles should be read with that in mind. In the same way, orderings that change one line at a time, Gray, 先天 in long stretches, the palaces of 八宫 and the 消息 tide, keep five of six bells sounding from step to step, so under six voices they ring true almost by construction; the interesting cells there are the orderings that jump. The two-octave fold and the undertone series are left out of the table because the rating listens to intervals regardless of octave and direction: they score exactly as the chromatic ruler and the harmonic series do, though they sound different and are worth hearing in the laboratory. And the scale of consonance is one ear. A cell that is gold under it is a place to listen first, never a verdict.

06 · Listening notes

What your ear keeps.

Anything kept from the laboratory appears here with room for a note. The notes stay in this browser. The box at the end gathers them as plain text, to copy out and bring back to the conversation.

Return

Does a phrase come back? An ordering that revisits a pitch region after wandering gives the ear a home. The 卦气 year and the 消息 tide are built to return; listen for whether the assignment lets that be heard.

Cadence

Where does motion come to rest? In the couplets of the 序卦 the second hexagram often answers the first. Under some assignments the answer is a fall of a fifth, under others a leap, under others a repeated note.

Distance

A single moving line can be a semitone or two octaves, depending on which line it is and how the lines weigh. Listen to whether the size of a change matches its weight in the reading.

Grain

Some assignments make every hexagram distinct; others let many share one sound. When hexagrams collide, the ordering becomes a smaller tune in a larger cycle. Listen for which collisions feel like kinship.

07 · Still wondering

Open as the laboratory is left.

Wondering

京房's two instruments

The same name is attached to the sixty pitches, the 卦气 calendar and the eight palaces. Whether these were ever heard together, a calendar of hexagrams sounding on a chain of sixty fifths, is a question for the texts. The laboratory can at least play what that would sound like.

Wondering

The bottom line or the top

Weighting the bottom line finest reads a hexagram from its root; weighting the top finest reads it from its crown. The survey lets the two be compared through every ordering. If one consistently rings truer, it says something about where the tradition places the slow and the quick.

Wondering

A tuning per ordering

Perhaps no single assignment serves every score. The 卦气 year may want the chain of fifths, the 序卦 the harmonic series, a life on bamboo the six bells. If the ear lands there, the finding is that the orders were made for different instruments.

Sources and method
《吕氏春秋 · 音律》The rule of 三分损益, generating the twelve pipes by alternately adding and removing a third of the length.
京房六十律Recorded in the 律历志 of the 后汉书: the chain of fifths extended to sixty pitches. Used here as the third ruler, by analogy.
京房 八宫Eight palaces of eight: the pure hexagram, five generations changing one line upward at a time, the wandering soul and the returning soul. Generated by rule here.
卦气The year of sixty hexagrams from 中孚 at 冬至, as in note 09.
郑玄 爻辰Lines assigned to the twelve pipes, the source of the whole-tone line bells used in notes 08–11.
Baoshan and 左传 castsAs in notes 11 and 09: each cast heard as its first hexagram followed by its second.
Consonance scaleInterval classes 0–6 rated 1, 0, 0.3, 0.7, 0.75, 0.9, 0.05, as in note 10. Pitches off the twelve notes are rounded to the nearest semitone for rating only; they sound unrounded.
MethodResonance is the mean rating over all pairs of pitches in the union of each pair of neighbouring steps. The percentile compares the real ordering with 300 random shuffles of its own steps, using a fixed random seed so the table is the same on every visit.
