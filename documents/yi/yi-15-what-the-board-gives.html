What theboard gives

A weiqi board, flat and then in the round, used to ask where position comes from.

The last note ended on a pair: a square board that stays still, and round stones that move. Every historical reader took the board as given. This note changes it. First the flat board is given a way to read itself, so that every point carries six lines. Then the board is bent into a cylinder, a torus and a sphere, and the same stones are read again. Three small experiments follow, each one something a person can do with a finger and then put into words. The mathematics stays in the margins.

位wèi
位 is place: where a line stands in the stack of six, where a stone stands on the board. The 易 judges a line by its place before anything else.
What this note finds
A place gets its meaning from three sources, and the experiments pull them apart: the ground it sits on, the relations it enters, and the moment at which it is read.

00 · Opening

The meaning of a move depends on what receives it.

Here is the outcome, before any of the work. A stone, or a line, means what it does because of four things, and they can be told apart.

01 · POSSIBILITY

The ground

The surface decides which relations are available at all. Corners, edges and seams exist before anyone acts.

02 · CONFIGURATION

The stones

Each stone takes a place and changes which relations actually hold.

03 · INTERPRETATION

The reading

A reading gives significance to the configuration, place by place.

04 · TIME

The moment

If the field keeps traces, two arrangements that look alike can carry different pasts.

This way of stating it came from ChatGPT, reading the experiments from outside after Lin Wei asked the question that brought them home: what does any of this mean to someone who practises the 易, or plays weiqi? The experiments had become finer than the question. This note puts them back in order of usefulness.

Three of the sections below are built around something to do. Move one yang line through the six places of a hexagram. Look at the same stones on two surfaces. Watch two orders of play arrive at one arrangement. Between them sit the two instruments that made the experiments possible: a flat board that reads itself, and the same board as a body that can be turned.

What this is. A model we designed, and a set of counts on a working text. The six-line reading of a board is our construction. Nothing here shows that it helps a player win or a practitioner read. Its value so far is that it lets the four things above be changed one at a time.
01 · First experiment

Move one yang line through six places.

Five yin lines and one yang. The proportions never change. Tap a place to put the yang there, and read what the book says about it.

At the bottom the single yang is 复, return: something has just come back and should be tended. At the top it is 剥, stripping away: the last fruit on the branch. In the second place it is the general in the middle of his army. In the fifth it is the king. The forces are identical in all six. What differs is where the one firm thing stands, and the book treats that as the whole story.

For someone who reads the 易, this is the oldest lesson there is, and it is worth feeling it in the hand. Before asking whether a line is strong or weak, ask where it is, and what stage of a process that place implies.

The received text bears this out by count. We coded all 384 line statements by whether they contain 吉 or 凶 and tallied them by place.

Of 64 lines at each place, how many statements carry 吉 and how many 凶. The pattern repeats in each trigram: bottom fair, middle best, top worst.

The second and fifth places, the middles of the two trigrams, are the fortunate ones. The third, at the top of the lower trigram, is the most exposed. The top line has as many warnings as blessings. The 系辞 says it in a line: 二多誉，四多惧，三多凶，五多功. The count agrees with three of the four, and the fourth place, the fearful one, turns out to have the fewest outright misfortunes of all.

One result surprised us. The tradition also teaches 当位, that a yang line belongs in an odd place and a yin line in an even one. By this count, lines in their proper place and lines out of it score the same: 0.172 each. The middle places score 0.37 against 0.07 for the rest. In the verdict words of the text, where a line sits matters, and whether it suits its seat does not show.

How far this goes. The text was reconciled from two public datasets, each with faults, and is short of a critical edition. The count uses two characters. It describes a pattern in the wording and gives no rule for any single reading. Scholars of the 易 have counted these things before, and we have not yet set our figures beside theirs.
02 · The flat board

A board that reads itself.

To compare a board with a hexagram, the board needs lines. This is the smallest construction we found that gives every point six of them.

Under the stones lies a field. A white stone holds its point at +1 and a black stone at −1. Every empty point settles to nine-tenths of the average of its neighbours. Influence fades with distance and gathers wherever the board gives it nowhere to leak away. Strong players carry something like this in their heads; modern weiqi programs compute a far better version and call it ownership.

Now stand on one point and look outward in rings. The first line of its hexagram is the point itself: which way does it lean? The second line is the ring of its four neighbours. The third, the ring two steps out. Then two wider rings, and for the sixth line the whole board, which every point shares. White-leaning reads as yang, black-leaning as yin. The lower trigram is the point and what touches it. The upper trigram is its surroundings.

Every point now has a hexagram, including the empty ones, and it changes as stones arrive. A line whose average sits close to zero is one that is about to turn, which is a workable picture of a 变爻.

Second experiment

Same stones, different ground. Six stones: three black in a corner, three white in the belly of the board, mirror images of each other. Before you change the surface, say which points you expect to change, and why. Then join the edges and look.

Gold leans white, green leans black. Dashed stubs mark edges that are joined to the opposite side. Tap any point to read it.

On the plane, the corner point reads 剥. Join the edges into a torus and it cannot complete a hexagram at all: the two groups have become exact mirror images, the whole board hangs in balance, and the sixth line has nothing to say. No stone moved. Thirty-seven of the eighty-one points changed their reading because the ground did.

The second position makes the point a weiqi player will feel at once. Two walls face each other. On the plane each wall owns the strip behind it, because the edge of the board closes the strip off. Join the left and right edges, and the two strips become one corridor running between the walls. Behind black's wall the field drops from −0.45 to −0.13. The wall did half the work. The board did the other half.

A question for players. When a corner move is called efficient, how much of the efficiency belongs to the stone, and how much to the board?

We guessed wrong here once, and the guess is worth keeping. We expected the corner group to tip the whole plane toward black. It tips very slightly toward white. The corner keeps a small region firmly. The belly group colours more of the board. Holding and reaching are different things, and the flat board cannot show why. For that the board has to be seen as a body.

03 · In the round

The board as a body.

Four bodies carry the same grid. Turn each one. First look at the ground alone, before any stone, then switch to seeds and drop some.

Body
How uneven is the ground · highest ÷ lowest
1.00× means every point is the same place.

To see what the ground gives before any stone lands, place one lone seed on a point and measure two things. Hold is how firmly the seed keeps the points right beside it. Reach is how much of the whole body it colours. Do that for every point and the body shows its own terrain.

So the plane already separates two roles before play begins. Near the edge a seed holds. A little further in it reaches. Weiqi teaching has names for exactly this pair: the third line is the line of territory, 地, and the fourth is the line of influence, 势. Our simple field puts both one line closer to the edge than the teaching does, and the reaching line moves when the damping changes. The separation itself stayed put every time we changed the settings.

On the torus the terrain is perfectly level. Every one of its 169 points has the same hold and the same reach. There is no corner to take and no edge to lean on. A wall that runs all the way round encloses nothing, because there is no inside for it to close. The first stone chooses no position. It makes one: until it lands, there is no here and no there.

The sphere will not lie level. A grid of squares cannot wrap a ball evenly, and this one gathers its unevenness into eight points with three neighbours each, where the corners of a cube used to be.

方 · PLANE

Place is given

Corners, sides and belly differ before play. A hexagram's six places work the same way: 初 has nothing beneath it, 上 nothing above, and the third and fourth lines meet at the seam between trigrams. The stone is judged by its place.

筒 · CYLINDER

Place and season

One direction keeps its two edges. The other closes into a cycle. The twelve 消息卦 have this shape: a bounded climb from the first line to the sixth, carried round the turning year.

环 · TORUS

Relation alone

Every point is the same place. What remains is how the stones stand to one another: 应, 比, 承, 乘. Position here is made entirely by what has been played.

球 · SPHERE

Place gathered into points

The edge is gone and its effect remains, concentrated at eight singular points. A closed, curved ground cannot share position out evenly.

Offered as a provocation. In the wider model of this sequence, 先天 is the fixed geometry and 后天 the calibration laid over it. The torus behaves like the uncalibrated surface. The plane behaves like the torus plus a standing bias toward whoever sits near the boundary. The edge acts as a kind of 后天. That is a way of seeing, and no classical source says it.
04 · The same bend, in one dimension

Ring the hexagram.

A hexagram is a very short board: six points in a row, with two ends. It can be bent as well.

Join the top line back to the bottom and the stack becomes a ring with no first place and no last. Hexagrams that differ only in where their pattern sits become one figure. This is the torus experiment again, on a board six points long.

Each ring reads from the marked bead, anticlockwise. Filled is yang, open is yin. The names beneath are the hexagrams that become that ring. The two gold-edged rings are mirror images of each other.

Sixty-four stacks make fourteen rings. The six hexagrams of the first experiment, 复 师 谦 豫 比 剥, are a single ring. Fifty of the sixty-four distinctions the book draws are distinctions of place and nothing else. Remove the two ends and they are gone.

ChatGPT added the next step in the margin, and the count holds. If the ring may also be turned over, fourteen become thirteen. Exactly one pair of rings are mirror images, and their twelve hexagrams turn out to be six neighbours in the received order of the book: 随 and 蛊, 噬嗑 and 贲, 困 and 井, 渐 and 归妹, 丰 and 旅, 涣 and 节.

The reduction is plain arithmetic. The interesting part is that the book refuses it. Two arrangements can be the same figure by one rule and entirely different situations by another, and the received text is where one can see which rule a tradition chose. Section 06 looks at that.

05 · Third experiment

Two histories, one arrangement.

The same eight seeds are placed on a torus in two opposite orders. Give the field less time to settle between moves and see what the order leaves behind.

The torus is shown unrolled. Outlined squares are points whose six lines differ between the two boards.

This experiment exists because ChatGPT challenged a claim. We had said that on the torus, position is made by history. It pointed out that our model has no memory. Two orders of play that end in the same arrangement give exactly the same reading. What the torus makes is position by relation. We withdrew the sentence.

History enters the moment the field is allowed to lag. With one step of settling between moves, 65 of the 169 points read differently when the last seed lands, though every seed is in the same place on both boards. With three steps, 25. With ten, four. Leave both boards alone and within about twenty steps they agree completely.

In this model, then, history is the part of the field that has yet to settle. A reading taken at rest describes a state. A reading taken in the middle of play describes a moment within a process, and still carries the order of what came before.

Both traditions already keep a little memory of this kind. A cast of the 易 is read as a pair, 本卦 and 之卦: where things stand and where they came from. And the ko rule of weiqi forbids recreating the position of one move ago. It is the single place where the rules of the game remember.

Scope. This is memory in one relaxation model, and it fades. The lasting kind, habits, scars, things that cannot be undone, would need a mechanism that survives equilibrium. The model has none yet.
06 · In the margin

What the received text keeps.

If place were only a convention, hexagrams that share a ring would have similar line statements. We tested that, and then tested every other symmetry the ring has. This section is the detailed part, kept brief.

The ring

No trace

Lines that sit on the same bead of the same ring are no more alike than lines shuffled at random, in their verdicts or in the images they use. The text has no memory of the ring.

The mirror

A clear trace

A hexagram and its reversal, the pair the tradition calls 综, share images far more often than chance allows. Searching blind, the three strongest echoes found were 损五 with 益二, 夬四 with 姤三, and 既济三 with 未济四. In each, a line answers the line at its mirror place.

We then tried all twenty-three ways of carrying one hexagram onto another by turning the ring, flipping it, or exchanging yin and yang. Only one leaves a mark in the text: the pairing of the received order itself, which sets each hexagram beside its reversal, and beside its complement in the four cases where reversal changes nothing.

That result has a limit we could not get past. Every reversal pair is also a pair of neighbours in the book. The test cannot say whether the echo belongs to the geometry or to the editors who composed the pairs side by side. Separating the two would take manuscript evidence in which the hexagrams are ordered differently, and that is a question for textual historians.

For this note the plain reading is enough. A board with edges keeps its mirror symmetries and loses its shifts. A torus keeps the shifts. By this test the book behaves like a board with edges. And the reversal pair is the most ordinary fact in weiqi: the same position, seen from the opponent's seat.

These are exploratory statistics on a working text: counts of two characters, overlap of shared characters after common words are removed, and shuffles within each place. Twenty-three symmetries were tried at once, which calls for caution about any single figure. The detailed numbers and the scripts sit with the companion instrument, Board as Body.
07 · Close

What it means, and for whom.

We changed the shape of a board and ended up asking where meaning lives. The experiments make four answers distinguishable. That is their use.

For someone who reads the 易
Three things to carry into a reading

Place first

  • Weigh where a line stands before asking whether it is correct there. The verdicts of the text follow the first and are silent on the second.
  • Read a hexagram together with its reversal, as one situation seen from two seats.
  • A cast is a moment inside a process. That is why it comes as a pair.
For someone who plays weiqi
Language more than technique

What the board gives for free

  • Territory exists because the board has an edge. Take the edge away and nearly everything remains influence.
  • Holding and reaching are properties of the ground before any stone is played.
  • Nothing here will improve anyone's game. It may change how a familiar corner looks.
For this sequence
One idea to take back

位 as boundary

  • Note 13 treats a hexagram as a field. A field needs a boundary, and 位 may be it: the two ends and the seam of a six-place stack.
  • Both the book and the board are edge systems. Meaning comes from where the edges are.
  • Remove them and only relation is left, which is the loom of note 06.
位
One sentence to carry forward

Possibility is shaped by structure, meaning comes through position and relation, and time decides what remains of an intervention.

Established
  • On a torus every point has the same hold and reach. On a plane they differ, and peak on different lines.
  • The same six stones give different readings at 37 of 81 points when a nine-line plane becomes a torus.
  • The 64 hexagrams form 14 rings under rotation and 13 if the ring may be turned over.
  • In our working text, verdicts follow place, with the middle of each trigram most favoured.
Provisional
  • The field is a simple damped relaxation. It knows nothing of life and death.
  • Which ring feeds which line is a choice. So is white as yang.
  • The sixth line is shared by the whole board, so at most 32 hexagrams can appear at once. A different sixth line would lift that.
  • The text counts rest on a rough corpus and have not been set against the scholarship.
Open
  • Does the six-line reading of a board mean anything to a player? Untested.
  • Is the echo between reversal pairs geometry or editing?
  • Why does the top of each trigram fare worst? The seam and the edge may be one thing.
Withdrawn
  • "On the torus, position is made by history." It is made by relation. Order leaves a mark only while the field is unsettled.
  • "The corner group tips the board toward black." It tips slightly toward white. The corner holds; the belly reaches.
  • Nested discs for the six lines. They agreed by construction and showed two hexagrams on a whole board. Rings replaced them.

The board strand rests here. It began with a patent and a hunch, passed through two instruments and a night of counting, and comes back with one idea that the rest of the sequence can use: that a place is an edge condition before it is anything else.
