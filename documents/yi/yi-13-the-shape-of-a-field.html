The shapeof a field

How a bead becomes a field, and what a field can carry: pitch, slope, tide and answer.

Every hexagram can be given a field, a +1 or −1 laid over all sixty-four situations; note 07, which follows, works these fields out in full. This note asks what shape such fields have. The cube that holds the sixty-four rings like a struck body, and each of the sixty-four fields is one of its standing waves, pitched by the number of lines it listens to. Heat poured onto a single hexagram spreads into a field, and the three coins of a cast spread it for an exact, nameable length of time. A flow across the sixty-four parts cleanly into a slope and a tide, and the slope is 势. Two fields can answer one another, each becoming part of the other's ground, and that answer is a working form of 感应.

00 · Opening

A field has a shape, and the shape can be read.

The loom gave the sixty-four one body. This note looks at what lies across that body, the field, the way an architect looks at a site: its pitch, its slopes, its currents, and how it meets its neighbours. The listening chapters that follow hear the same fields as chords, tides and scores.

Graph theory sees the sixty-four as beads joined by threads: sixty-four vertices, each joined to the six that differ from it by one line. That picture holds every adjacency the earlier chapters relied on, and it says little about what lies across the beads. A field is what lies across them. It gives every situation a value at once, so it can have a slope, a ridge, a hollow and a current.

Mathematics has a mature way to carry fields onto a graph. Values on the beads are potentials. Values on the threads are flows. Values on the small squares that four beads make are whirls. Three operators move between them, the discrete cousins of gradient, divergence and curl, and with them the geometry of smooth fields arrives intact on a finite set of sixty-four. The sections below use those tools four times. First to hear the cube ring. Then to melt a bead into a field. Then to split a current into slope and tide. Then to let two fields answer.

形而上者谓之道，
形而下者谓之器。
What is above form is called the Way; what is below form is called the vessel.《周易 · 系辞上》 · translation by the author

The fields of this note sit at that seam. They have shape, so they belong to form, and each one is a way of reading every situation together, which is closer to a way than to a thing.

01 · Standing waves

The sixty-four fields are the cube's own vibrations.

Strike a bell and it rings in a set of shapes that belong to the bronze. The six-line cube has such shapes too, and there are exactly sixty-four of them: the Walsh fields of note 07.

The instrument that finds a body's vibrations is its Laplacian. At every situation it asks how far the field there stands from the field at the six neighbouring situations, and adds up the differences. A field that the Laplacian only rescales, without changing its shape, is a standing wave, and the scale factor is its pitch.

On the cube the result is clean. The field of hexagram kk is a standing wave whose pitch is twice the number of its yang lines. The reason fits in a sentence: at every situation, exactly as many of its six neighbours read the opposite sign as there are lines that field listens to. 坤 listens to none and is the steady drone. 复, 师, 谦, 豫, 比 and 剥 each listen to one line and form the lowest audible band. 乾 listens to all six and sounds highest, flipping sign at every step.

01 · The rack

Seven bands, and the lantern is the score.

Group the fields by pitch and the bands hold 1, 6, 15, 20, 15, 6 and 1. Those are the lantern's rings. The same seven rings count situations by their yang lines and count fields by their pitch.

+1−1selected
Tap a ring to strike its bell. Tap a bead or a cell to choose a hexagram.
You are hearing chime-bells modelled on the 曾侯乙编钟 (Marquis Yi of Zeng, 433 BCE), struck singly or as a group. The seven bands are seven bells on 宫 商 角 徵 羽 and the 宫 and 商 above, 坤's drone the deepest. Struck together, they sound at once.
01 · computed

Pitch counts lines

For every hexagram k and every situation, exactly |k| of the six neighbours carry the opposite sign. The Laplacian returns the field multiplied by 2|k|, everywhere, all sixty-four at once.

02 · computed

The lantern is the spectrum

The bands hold 1, 6, 15, 20, 15, 6, 1 fields, the binomial row of six. Note 05 found the same numbers as rings of situations by yang count. One body serves as the elevation of the situations and the score of the fields.

03 · computed

Two kinds of frequency

复 and 剥 share a pitch on the cube: each listens to one line. Along note 07's thread of 64 beats, 复 turns once and 剥 turns sixty-three times. Space hears how many lines a field listens to; time hears which ones.

04 · computed

坤 is the drone

The constant field has pitch zero. Under any spreading, smoothing or forgetting that the cube allows, it is the one voice that never fades, and every other voice settles toward it.

02 · From bead to field

Pour heat on one hexagram, and wait.

A single dial joins the old reading to the new. At time zero the hexagram is a bead, all weight on one situation. As time runs, the weight spreads along the lines and the bead becomes a field, with its peak still on the name it started from.

Heat on the cube is the gentlest way to spread. Each line, on its own clock, has some chance of having turned; the six lines turn independently, so the field at any moment is a product of six simple factors. In the spectrum the effect is a fade: every band loses amplitude at a rate set by its pitch, so the high bands go quiet first and 坤's drone stays at full strength. A sharp bead holds every pitch equally, like the click of a struck stick. A long-spread field is almost pure drone.

On level ground the peak never moves. For every moment after the start, the most likely situation is the one the heat was poured on, and the field falls away evenly with each line of difference. The name keeps its place at the summit while the field around it widens. That is one way to keep a threshold real inside a field: the named hexagram persists as the peak of a landscape that now reaches everywhere.

The three-coin cast turns out to be exactly this heat, run for a definite time. Three coins make each line move with chance one in four, independently of the others and of whether the line is yin or yang. The spread of possible 之卦 around a cast 本卦 is therefore the heat field at the moment when one line in four has turned: t=12ln⁡2t = \tfrac12\ln 2. At that moment every band's amplitude has halved once per line it listens to, so the spectrum descends in octaves, 1, ½, ¼, ⅛ and on.

The milfoil differs in a way the field makes visible. In the yarrow-stalk count a yang line moves with chance three in eight and a yin line with one in eight. Its rate of forgetting is the same as the coins': both per-line matrices have second eigenvalue one half, so the two methods cast for the same length of time. The milfoil's ground is tilted. Left to run, it would settle with yin three times as likely as yang in every line, which makes its change a diffusion down a gentle slope, the height of each situation rising by ln 3 for each yang line. The milfoil's change is all 势: a reversible spread on a landscape whose lowest point is 坤.

02 · The dial

Turn the dial and the bead melts.

Choose a hexagram, then move time forward. The board shows where the weight has gone, the loom shows it as a hill on one continuous skin, the bars show what remains of each band, and the bells sound the field as one chord.

05 · computed

Where the peak stays

On level ground the starting hexagram carries the most weight for all time. On the milfoil's slope the peak holds through cast time and moves to 坤 once t passes ½ ln 3 ≈ 0.549, when a yang line becomes likelier to have turned than not.

06 · computed

Three coins are a length of time

The distribution of 之卦 under the coin cast equals heat on the cube at t = ½ ln 2 ≈ 0.347, exactly, for all sixty-four starting hexagrams. Each band's amplitude is 2−b.

07 · computed

Milfoil and coins share a clock

Per line, the coin matrix and the milfoil matrix both have eigenvalues 1 and ½. They forget at the same rate. They differ only in where forgetting leads.

08 · computed

The milfoil's ground slopes

The milfoil chain is reversible, with long-run odds of 3 : 1 for yin in each line. It is diffusion on a landscape of height ln 3 per yang line, pure slope with no circulation, lowest at 坤. Left to rest, its field is exactly the three-coin cast of 坤.

03 · Slope and tide

Every current is a slope plus a tide.

Put a flow on the threads, any flow at all: how readily situations pass into their neighbours. It splits in exactly one way into a part that runs downhill and a part that goes round. The first is 势. The second is the tide of the twelve 消息卦.

The split is the Hodge decomposition, and on the cube it has two parts. The gradient part is the flow you would get from a landscape alone, water finding its way down: a pure propensity, carried by the ground. The circulation part goes round the small squares of the cube and returns where it began; no landscape can make it, because water running downhill never comes back up to its source. The two parts are perpendicular, so every flow's strength divides cleanly between them, and neither can borrow from the other.

This settles something the earlier model only suspected. A single landscape cannot produce the returning tide of the 消息卦, 复 rising through 临, 泰, 大壮, 夬 to 乾, then 姤 falling through 遁, 否, 观, 剥 back to 坤. That tide is pure circulation: its slope part is exactly zero. A model that wants both the propensity and the return needs both parts, and the decomposition hands each one back separately, so either can be measured, drawn and heard on its own.

The tool is also kind to computation. The landscape hidden in any flow is found by solving one equation with the Laplacian of section 01, and because the Walsh fields are its standing waves, the solve is a Walsh transform, a division by pitch, and a transform back. The fields of note 07 are the natural coordinates of 势.

A third part, on the loom. On the cube there is no third part, because the cube has no holes: every loop can be filled with squares. Drawn on note 06's torus, with the threads that run around it and its sixty-four squares, the same flows gain exactly two more directions, the windings around the torus's two circles. Those are the two winding rates that the torus dynamics measured, lower trigram and upper. Whether a returning current counts as a local whirl or a global winding depends on which squares are filled, and that is a choice of model the loom makes visible.

What kind of picture this is. A field here is a value on every one of the sixty-four at once, and the arithmetic lives on those sixty-four. The skin between them is drawn for the eye. The cube has six dimensions and these bodies three, so neither can set all six neighbours of every hexagram beside it. On the loom four of the six lie beside it, and the other two are chords, three places away round a ring; a spark on a chord skips over the weave, fainter. On the lantern fewer than three of the six sit among a bead's nearest, so a field there shows as scattered lights. The loom is the picture of the field. The lantern is the picture of its measure, and of the tide, which climbs it from 坤 to 乾 and returns.
04 · Answering

Two fields answer by multiplying.

感应 asks that situations feel one another. Fields can do this in a precise way: multiplied point by point, two Walsh fields always make a third, and the third is named by the lines where the first two differ.

Wherever two fields carry the same sign, their product is +1; wherever they part, −1. The product of hexagram aa's field and hexagram bb's is the field of the hexagram whose yang lines mark where aa and bb differ. In acoustics two tones heard together make sum and difference tones. In a binary world addition and subtraction coincide, so the sum tone and the difference tone are one tone, and it is this one.

Read through a cast, the rule has a direct meaning. Multiply the field of the 本卦 by the field of the 之卦 and the result is the field of the moving lines themselves, the change written as a hexagram. It reads +1 on the thirty-two situations where before and after agree and −1 on the thirty-two where they part. When no line moves, the product is 坤, the drone: before and after agree everywhere.

Recorded casts
The third field is the first two multiplied, situation by situation. Casts from the 左传 and 国语 as listed in note 07. Tap a line to switch it between yin and yang; tap its marker to set it moving.

Multiplication gives the vocabulary; a dynamics needs a mechanism. The one proposed here keeps 感应 inside the ground. Each situation carries a field of weight, as in section 02, spreading on a landscape, as in section 03. Let the landscape of one field include the other field, so that weight in one draws the other toward the same places. Then neither field forces the other from outside. Each becomes part of the other's ground, and they can fall into step together. The torus dynamics found no such falling into step, and it found the reason too: the 后天 context entered there as an outside push. Coupling through the ground is the change that opens the door.

Still wondering

Where the arithmetic stops, the wondering starts.

What follows is reflection. It is fenced off from the ledger on purpose.

The milfoil's ground slopes toward 坤. Cast after cast, if its change were left to run, every line would lean three to one toward yin, and the field would gather at the receptive ground. 坤厚载物: the earth, thick enough to carry all things. A practice two and a half thousand years old has the shape of a landscape built into its arithmetic, and the coins, a later and simpler practice, levelled that landscape flat while keeping exactly the same clock. The two meet at one point: where the milfoil comes to rest is precisely the field the coins draw around 坤.

A name, on this reading, is the peak of a field. 屯 is where the weight is greatest, and the field of 屯 reaches every one of the sixty-four, thinner with each line of distance. The tradition's long habit of reading a cast through its neighbours, its 互卦, 错卦 and 综卦, its moving lines and the hexagram it moves toward, reads like a practice of feeling the field around the peak.

And the cube rings. Its seven bands are the lantern's seven rings, and its lowest voice, the one that never fades, is 坤. A situation struck sharply sounds every bell; a situation held long settles into the drone. Somewhere between the click and the hum is the time a cast takes, one half of the natural logarithm of two, during which one line in four has turned.
