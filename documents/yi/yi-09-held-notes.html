Held notesand rests

Two scores of the Yi, read in time.

Music is heard in intervals and durations as much as in pitches: how long a note is held, how long the silence lasts before the next. The Yi's records carry time too. One score writes its note values down — the Han 卦气 year, where each hexagram governs six days and seven-eightieths. Another lets them be read off history — the casts of the 左传 and 国语, each dated, each followed by what came of it. This note sets the two side by side and listens.

00 · Duration

A chord is also a length of time.

The previous notes gave the hexagrams pitch: fields, fifths, voices leaning toward their turn. A song also needs to know how long each chord sounds, and how long the quiet lasts between phrases.

Two kinds of record answer this differently. A composed score assigns its durations in advance: the 卦气 calendar gives each hexagram a span of days, and the tide of the twelve 消息卦 gives each a month. A performed score has to be read backwards: a cast was made on a date, something followed on a later date, and the distance between them is how long that chord was held in a life or a state. The first is exact and arbitrary; the second is uncertain and lived. Both are played below.

01 · The 卦气 year

Sixty notes of six days and seven-eightieths.

In the Han 卦气 system attributed to 孟喜 and developed by 京房, sixty hexagrams take turns over the year — 分六十四卦，更直日用事 — each holding for 6 7/80 days, so that sixty of them fill 365¼. 中孚 opens at the winter solstice; 咸 at the summer.

The four remaining hexagrams, 坎, 震, 离 and 兑, are set apart as the four 正卦 of the seasons. Their twenty-four lines govern the twenty-four 节气, one line for each fortnight. The year therefore sounds in two tempi at once: a melody of sixty chords changing every six days, over a bass of twenty-four lines changing every fifteen. The twelve sovereign hexagrams of the tide, marked in the ring, recur inside the melody one in every five.

02 · The 左传 score

Each cast held until its answer arrived.

Fifteen recorded casts from the 左传 and 国语, each drawn as a held note from the day it was cast to the day its outcome came. Time runs on a logarithmic scale, so a battle and a dynasty fit in one score.

Softened ends mark the range where the dates are uncertain. The notes fall into four registers, and the register follows the question. Battles were asked about in days, campaigns in months, marriages and revolts in years, births in a lifetime, and the fortunes of a house in centuries. The person casting chooses the length of the song they are asking about.

03 · Felt time

The Spring and Autumn, played in felt time.

Strict time cannot be played: one note would last three hundred years. Felt time folds each duration onto a logarithm, as memory does — a day is short, a decade long, a dynasty longer, but not a hundred thousand times longer.

The casts play in the order they were made. Each 本卦 sounds for its felt duration, the moving bells are struck again on their new tones as it ends, and the next cast follows after a rest scaled the same way to the years between them. The tuning is the one chosen above.

04 · Uncertain time

Where the durations are guessed.

Every held note here is a reading of the record, and some are readings of readings.

Casts told in retrospect. Several casts appear in the record years after they were made, at the moment their outcome arrived. The cast for 伯姬's marriage is told after the battle at 韩; 穆姜's at her death; 叔孙豹's at his. Their start dates are inferred from the events they concern — a wedding, a confinement, a birth — and carry ranges.

Prophecies fulfilled centuries later. The two longest notes, 陈完's and 毕万's, belong to the kind of record most likely shaped after the fact. Their length may be the narrator's rather than the diviner's. They are drawn at full length and marked as such.

Outcomes that unfold. A house rising, a ruler governing: some answers arrive gradually. Where an outcome has stages, the range runs from the first to the last.

Rests that were never recorded. The silences between casts are only the silences of the surviving record. Many casts were made and never written down; the rests in this song are partly the record's forgetting.

05 · Still wondering

Threads left open, on purpose.

Wondering

The asker sets the bar

If the length of a song is chosen by the question, then the same hexagram held for a day and for a dynasty is the same chord in two tempi. Does its meaning change with the tempo?

Wondering

Two tempi in one year

The 卦气 year runs a six-day melody over a fifteen-day bass. Part Two found the lines behaving as clocks at different rates. Is the calendar a slower instance of the same layered time?

Wondering

Dwell and duration

The dynamical model measured how long a state dwells before a line turns. The records measure how long a cast was held before it was answered. These may be the same quantity seen from inside and from outside.

Held lightly

Felt time is a choice

The logarithm is one way of folding time, close to how memory compresses it. Others are possible; each would make a different song from the same record.
