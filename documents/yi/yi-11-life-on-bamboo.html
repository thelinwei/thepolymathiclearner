易 Yi · 11 · Field note · October 2026 · A bounded life

A lifeon bamboo

Six casts for 邵𬊤, 318–316 BCE, struck on the bells.

In 316 BCE a high officer of the state of Chu was buried at Baoshan with the records of the divinations made for him in his last years. They are working records, not stories told afterwards: dated to the day, naming the diviner and the instrument, giving the question, the numbers the milfoil produced, the reading, and the remedy. Six of them give their results as numbers. In sequence they make a short song with a known ending: two phrases a year apart, then four on a single day, and in each the person asking set the length of the phrase.

00 · The man

邵𬊤, Commander of the Left.

Shao Tuo was 左尹, Commander or Superintendent of the Left, a senior judicial officer at the Chu court. His tomb near Jingmen in Hubei was opened in 1987 and held 278 inscribed bamboo strips: court cases, an inventory of the grave goods, and records of divination and prayer.

The divinations run through the last three years of his life, from the early summer of 318 BCE to the early summer of 316, when he died. Some were made with turtle shell, some with milfoil. They asked the same two questions again and again: will he serve the king this coming year without trouble, and will his illness heal? Six of the milfoil records give their results as numbers, and all six are the score here. One comes from 318, one from 317, and four were cast on a single day in 316.

The transcriptions and dates follow Constance Cook's edition of the Baoshan divination text, which places each record in a dated sequence of events. Her six transcriptions contain exactly thirty-four 一, thirty-one 六, six 八 and one 五: the same tally Edward Shaughnessy gives independently for all numbers in the Baoshan records, which is a useful check that nothing has been dropped or misread.
01 · Form of a record

A question, numbers, a reading, a remedy.

Every Chu milfoil record follows the same course, in two steps. The first asks broadly and finds a trouble. The second proposes how to remove it, and is answered simply: auspicious.

01

The date

A year named for an event, a month, a day. 荆夷之月，己卯之日.

02

Diviner and charge

Who cast, with which method, and what was asked: "throughout the year, would that his person have no trouble."

03

The numbers

Two columns of six numbers, written side by side down the strip and read from right to left.

04

The reading

恒贞吉，少有忧于… The long-term outlook is auspicious, but there is a little concern in…

05

The remedy

Prayers and offerings to dispel the trouble, divined again, and found 吉.

The charge itself carries a duration. "From the 荆夷 month all the way until the next 荆夷 month, throughout the entire year." The person asking sets the length of the phrase before the stalks are counted. This is the observation from note 09, written into the question in the asker's own words: the bar is a year long because a year is what was asked about.

The numbers are written top to bottom, with the bottom numeral as the first line. They were read with a Chu method like the one in the Tsinghua manuscript 筮法, which reads the two columns as four trigrams in four positions rather than as one hexagram changing into another. Here, odd numbers are struck at a bell's face as yang and even numbers at its side as yin, so the casts can be heard on the same bells as the rest of the sequence; the single 五 is heard as yin, as both editors read it. The names of 周易 hexagrams under each column are the ones Cook gives, offered for orientation, not as the Chu diviners' reading.
02 · Six casts

Two years of service, then one long day.

The numbers are shown as they stand on the strip, the first column on the right. Strike each cast to hear its two columns as two chords on the bells.

316 BCE, early summer, 荆夷之月, the day 己卯. Two diviners, each with his own method, put the same two questions on the same day: the year ahead, and the illness. Four casts, set out here as a square, the questions in rows and the diviners in columns.

You are hearing chime-bells modelled on the 曾侯乙编钟: odd numbers at the face, even numbers at the side, a minor third higher. The voice is drawn from recorded strikes of bronze bells, small and great: a bright clang, then a hum that blooms and rings on, longest in the lowest bells.
03 · The song

Two phrases, a long rest, a quartet, and a bell.

Played in order: the yearly cast of 318, the yearly cast of 317, then the four casts of the single day in 316, close together as they were made. A single low bell follows for the death that came that summer.

The readings darken across the three years. In 318 there is some concern for his person, and his promotion is slow. In 317 there is concern for his person and his home and something discordant outside, yet after the remedy the diviner adds three years without trouble and great joy, of which the country will know. On the day in 316 both diviners find concern in the home, one of them naming the bedroom, and both find the illness changed for the worse: slow to heal, says one; more serious, says the other. Each reading still opens with 恒贞吉, "the long-term outlook is auspicious", and each remedy is still answered 吉. The form keeps its composure while the content turns.

04 · What holds

Different numbers, the same reading.

The single day in 316 is the clearest thing the record lets us hear. Two methods, two diviners, two pairs of numbers for each question, with nothing in common between them. And the readings agree.

Heard

The numbers diverge

For the year, 陈乙's columns read 蛊 and 剥, 五生's read 随 and 离. For the illness, 颐 and 无妄 against 恒 and 需. No column repeats, and on the bells the four phrases share no chord.

Heard

The readings converge

Both yearly casts find concern in the home. Both illness casts find the sickness changed for the worse. Whatever each method saw in its numbers, the two arrived at the same place, which suggests the reading drew on more than the numbers alone: the person, the season, the illness already known.

Heard

How much moves

Between a cast's two columns, between one and three lines turn: three in 318, one in 317, and two, three, two and three on the day in 316. No pattern in so few phrases, but the one quiet movement belongs to the year the diviner promised great joy.

A coincidence, kept as one. A reader of the 周易 will notice that 陈乙's illness cast opens on 颐, "nourishment", the hexagram of the mouth and of feeding, cast for a man who had lost his appetite: 不甘食. The Chu diviners did not read with the 周易, and nothing on the strip makes the connection. The resemblance belongs to us, not to the record, and it is set down here so that it can be seen and set aside.
Retracted. An earlier draft of this note, built on three records whose transcriptions came from more than one edition with different column orders, heard the bottom three numbers holding steady inside every cast, like a drone. With all six records from one consistent edition, that pattern does not hold: two casts share a lower trigram, one an upper, and three share nothing. The drone was an artefact of mixed transcriptions.
05 · Still wondering

Threads left open, on purpose.

Wondering

Why four on one day?

Two methods asked twice on the same day: a second opinion, a ritual completeness, or two specialists answering to the same household? The record keeps the practice and not the reason.

Wondering

Composure of the form

Every reading opens "auspicious" and every remedy closes "auspicious", even as the illness worsens. Is the form a way of holding a life steady while it changes?

Wondering

The 筮法 reading

Read by the Tsinghua method, as four trigrams in four positions, the same numbers might yield the home and the illness directly. That reading is the next thing to learn.

Wondering

The asker's bar

"From this month to the same month next year": the charge sets the duration before the cast. Notes 09 and 11 meet here. The length of a song is chosen by the one who asks.

Next score

The dynasties, slowed

From one man's three years to Shao Yong's ages of the world: the 皇极经世 as the longest composed score.

Held lightly

Odd and even

Striking odd at the face and even at the side follows the convention modern scholars use. The Chu diviners may have heard their numbers differently.
