01 · 术 shù

Procedure over Proof 以术代理

The axiomatic line, from Euclid onward, builds on axioms and chains of deduction. Its guiding question: "Does this hold, from first principles?"

The procedural line, from the 九章算术 onward, builds on shù: method, procedure, algorithm. Its guiding question: "Does this procedure always yield the correct result?"

Both lines are rigorous, and they find rigour in different places. The procedural line finds it in the operational invariance of a method across all valid inputs, much like a correctness proof for a computer program today. Modern mathematics uses both every day.

02 · 算筹 suàn chóu

A Grid-Based Notation 算筹符号

On this road the foundation is counting-rod notation: a base-10 positional system using vertical and horizontal rod arrangements, placed on a physical grid.

Each cell position carries the semantic weight. The layout itself is the syntax. Unknowns are positions in an array, where symbolic algebra would write x or y.

03 · 方程术 fāngchéng shù

Array Algebra 方程术

On the counting board a system of equations is laid out as columns of rods. Each column is one condition. Reading down a column you meet the count of each unknown in turn, and at the foot the total, called 实 shí. The first condition sits on the right, and the columns are read from right to left.

The board is then worked like a matrix. Whole columns are multiplied and subtracted from one another until a staircase of empty places appears and the last unknown can be read off. The 九章算术, compiled by around the first century CE, sets this out as fāngchéng shù, "the procedure of rectangular arrays", the method now called Gaussian elimination.

European mathematicians arrived at the same method independently, from Newton to Gauss. The two routes now meet in every linear-algebra library, and in every spreadsheet that solves a system of equations.

Below is the first problem of the chapter, worked exactly as the text instructs. Step through it to see the board change one move at a time. Every number stays a whole number until the very last step.

《九章算术》卷八 · 方程 · 第一题

今有上禾三秉，中禾二秉，下禾一秉，实三十九斗；上禾二秉，中禾三秉，下禾一秉，实三十四斗；上禾一秉，中禾二秉，下禾三秉，实二十六斗。问上、中、下禾实一秉各几何？

Three bundles of top-grade grain, two of middle and one of low yield 39 dou. Two, three and one yield 34. One, two and three yield 26. How much does one bundle of each grade yield?

Units are written with upright rods and tens with lying rods, alternating place by place; an empty place is a zero. The answer the text gives: top grade 9¼ dou, middle 4¼, low 2¾. Source: 《九章算术·方程》, Chinese Text Project.

04 · 出入相补 chūrù xiāng bǔ

Proof by Transformation 出入相补

Liu Hui's principle: "What goes out must equal what comes in." A geometric identity is proved by dissecting and rearranging until the equality becomes visually self-evident.

It works as a formal rewrite system: each dissection step is constrained by rules of area-preservation, and the final arrangement constitutes the proof.

Matured, this becomes a transformation logic, whose unit of reasoning is an area-preserving operation on a structure. It complements deductive logic, whose unit is an implication between propositions.

05 · 穷竭 qióng jié

Constructive Calculus 算法微积

Here the limit is an explicit procedure: exhaustion as algorithm. A derivative is the procedure "take the difference quotient, then narrow the interval." A real number is a program that can generate any desired decimal digit on demand. This is the constructive view that modern computable analysis also takes, alongside the Dedekind cut of classical analysis.

In the 5th century Zu Chongzhi placed π between 3.1415926 and 3.1415927 by running Liu Hui's polygon-inscribing procedure further. Carried forward, the same procedure is a natural foundation for analysis.

06 · 算板语言

The Abacus as Programming Language 算板语言

If the grid layout is the syntax and rod movements are the operations, the counting board is a two-dimensional programming language. Each computation is a sequence of legal moves on the grid.

What we call a function is a 术 shù, a named procedure. What we call a variable is a column position. What we call a proof is a demonstration that a sequence of moves always produces the desired configuration.

The board has a small instruction set, and the 方程术 from section 03 is a short program written in it. Set out line by line, the classical sentence and the modern line describe the same moves. Tap a line to watch it run on the board above.

Developed further, this tradition points toward formal rewriting systems, symbolic computation and something close to combinatory logic: the territory that Schönfinkel, Church and Turing mapped from the logical side in the twentieth century. The procedural and axiomatic roads meet in the modern computer.

  2. 以右行上禾遍乘中行middle ← middle × right.top
  3. 而以直除。while middle.top ≠ 0: middle ← middle − right
  4. 又乘其次，亦以直除。left ← left × right.top while left.top ≠ 0: left ← left − right
  5. 然以中行中禾不尽者遍乘左行而以直除。left ← left × middle.mid while left.mid ≠ 0: left ← left − middle
  6. 左方下禾不尽者，上为法，下为实。实即下禾之实。法 ← left.low # 36 实_low ← left.shi # 99
  7. 求中禾，以法乘中行下实，而除下禾之实。余如中禾秉数而一，即中禾之实。实_mid ← 法 × middle.shi − middle.low × 实_low 实_mid ← 实_mid ÷ middle.mid

07 · 得失

What Each Road Brings 得失

Natural on the procedural road

  • Computation and reasoning are unified from the start: the symbol is executable.
  • Linear algebra, polynomial systems, and combinatorics share a natural common notation (the array).
  • Constructive mathematics is the default, so the foundations debates of 1900 would take a different shape.
  • Symbolic computation as a natural extension of traditional methods.
  • A geometric language of transformation and invariance.

Natural on the axiomatic road

  • Set theory and the concept of actual infinity.
  • The axiomatic method and the idea of a mathematical "structure" defined by relations.
  • A sharp separation between geometry and number, which the procedural road keeps fused as "computable shapes."
  • Formal logic as an independent discipline.

A fully grown Chinese mathematics would be an edifice of its own, as rigorous as Euclid's and built on different ground. In it, operation and proof are inseparable, and the abacus board stands beside the straight-edge and compass as an instrument of abstract thought. Mathematics today already runs on both roads: proofs rest on the axiom, computers on the procedure. Holding the two together gives a fuller picture of what mathematical thinking can be.
