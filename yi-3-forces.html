Chapter sevenThe seam

Five does not need to become eight.

A great deal of scholarly effort has gone into reconciling the five phases with the eight trigrams — mapping one onto the other, assigning phases to directions, explaining away the leftover. The effort assumes the two systems are counting the same kind of thing.

They are not. Eight is a count of configurations. Five is a count of transformations. Nobody demands that a map's legend have as many entries as its compass rose, and nothing is wrong with a system that describes eight places using five kinds of movement between them.

So read the five phases as verbs, not nouns. Not water, wood, fire, earth, metal as materials, but as five things that can be happening to a state:

水

Store

Receive, retain, damp, hold potential in reserve.

木

Branch

Commit at the point of decision; let motion carry through.

火

Amplify

Intensify, express, drive outward — easing off as the extreme is reached.

土

Integrate

Mediate, couple, make disparate parts cohere.

金

Consolidate

Select, prune, settle into definite form.

Written as mathematics, each phase is an operator — a rule that takes the current state and returns a direction to move in. A mixture of the five, with weights, gives a velocity at every point in the cube. That is a vector field, and it is the object the rest of this part is about.

How to read itFive fixed operators and five weights that sum to one. Each operator takes the current state and hands back a direction; the weighted sum gives a velocity at every point in the cube. That is the whole of 五行, as mathematics.

Chapter eightFour fields you can draw

What each phase does to the space.

Here is the useful surprise. Four of the five operators can be drawn as an ordinary field of arrows: give them a position, they hand back a direction. The fifth cannot, and that turns out to matter more than anything else in this project.

Start with the four that behave.

That last observation is worth pausing on, because it is the first place the mathematics says something the tradition also says. Under 金 alone, the eight regions become attractors. Consolidation makes your current configuration stable — which is exactly the classical character of 金: definition, settling, the thing that gives form and then defends it.

The one you cannot draw.

木 is different in kind. Branching is not a direction that depends on where you are — it is something that happens to motion already underway, at the moment a decision is live. So 木 reads the velocity, not the position:

Plate 09 · the operator you cannot draw
F_branch = 0.95 · sign(xdot) · exp( - x^2 / 0.55^2 )
How to read itTwo factors. The first reads the velocity, so 木 chooses no direction of its own — it takes the one you are already travelling. The second is a window around zero, so it acts only when a decision is actually live. Together: it removes the friction at the moment of commitment, and at no other moment.
ẋ the direction already takenx distance from the threshold0.55 the width of the window

This has a consequence you can see immediately, and it is the reason 木 ends up being the most consequential operator in the whole model: two situations that are almost identical, both hovering near a threshold, get driven to opposite and irreversible configurations. And it takes a startlingly small amount of 木 to do it.

木 does not decide. It commits whatever was already leaning.

Which is why prediction and 木 are enemies, and why the instrument in Part Four ends up measuring exactly that trade. But we are getting ahead: first, why there are five of these things at all.

Chapter nineWhy five

An argument that does not appeal to authority.

The number five usually gets defended by tradition, or by a gesture at the five visible planets. There is a better answer available, and it is combinatorial.

The system needs two relations on the same set of phases. 生, generating: each feeds the next, around a cycle. 克, overcoming: each checks another, and classically it checks the one two steps along that same cycle, i -> i+2 mod n. The second relation is what stops the first from running away.

So ask: for which n does that arrangement actually work? Two conditions. The overcoming relation must visit every phase in a single loop — otherwise some phases never get checked. And it must not simply be the generating cycle run backwards, or it carries no new information.

And now the second thing the pentagram is telling us, which most modern retellings drop. The classical claim was never that these five things exist. It was that they constrain each other, in that specific double cycle. So the five weights should not be five free knobs — they should push on one another.

Plate 10 · the mixture, turning itself
adot_i = r · a_i · ( fit_i - sum_j a_j · fit_j ), fit_i = a_(i-1) - a_(i-2)
How to read itEach weight grows in proportion to its generating parent and shrinks in proportion to its controller two steps back. The weights always sum to one, so this is motion on a simplex. It has a balanced fixed point and never returns to it.
parent = 生 one step backcontroller = 克 two steps backr the rate of turnover

The behaviour of that system is the point. It does not settle into a balanced mixture. It circulates — one phase dominant for a stretch, then handing off to the next, in the generating order, indefinitely.

A model in which perfect balance is unstable is more faithful to 五行 than one in which balance is the goal. And it yields something falsifiable: track the phase mixture of any real system over time, and its trajectory should circulate in the generating direction rather than converge.

Balance was never a resting place. It was an order of succession.

Two defects these figures found, and one of them is this project's own argument biting back

The rule set after Part Two was that every figure must make its claimed effect visibly large, because a tenfold error had hidden inside an effect too small to see. Building these three figures duly turned up two more problems, and both are worth recording rather than quietly patching.

火 was divergent, and it was contained the wrong way. Written as a straight outward push it drives the state to the wall, so the position was clamped and the velocity left alone — which is precisely the defect that ruined an earlier version of the simulation. A clamped position with a live velocity is a pinned state that reads as motion; you get numbers that move and mean nothing. 火 now eases off as it approaches an extreme, so every operator is inward-or-zero at the boundary and no clamp ever has to bind. If a model needs a fence, the model is wrong.

金 was discontinuous — and the first account of why that matters was wrong. Written as a pull toward the nearest pole using the sign of the coordinate, it reverses direction the instant a line crosses zero. The claim was that this made a resting state chatter. It does not: sign makes the pole term repulsive from zero, so the state picks a side rather than oscillating, and when the test was finally built it showed no chatter at all. The claim was plausible, undemonstrated, and already written down.

The real cost is non-reproducibility. A discontinuous field is not Lipschitz at the threshold planes, so the trajectory depends on the integration step. Forty runs started near the walls, integrated at step 0.06 and again at 0.015: with sign the worst coordinate diverged by 0.94 — nearly the width of the cube — and the two step sizes disagreed about the sign of a line eleven times out of two hundred and forty. That means eleven different hexagrams depending on nothing but the step size. With tanh the divergence falls to 0.15 and the sign disagreements to zero. Smoothed in both the figures here and, as of this pass, in the Part Four instrument.

And the good one. For Figure 12 the mixture was first built as a four-phase ring, on the grounds that four colours are easier to follow than five. It would not circulate. It sat there. Which is exactly what Chapter Nine predicts — at n = 4 the overcoming relation splits into two disconnected pairs, so there is no single checking cycle and nothing drives the succession. The figure was refuted by the chapter it sits under. Rarely has a bug report been more satisfying.

Chapter ten后天

The situation is also moving.

Everything so far is internal — the state and its own transformations. But the classical system has a second layer, and it is the one usually treated as decoration: the distinction between 先天 and 后天.

Read them as two epistemic layers rather than two historical arrangements. 先天 is the invariant geometry — the cube, the sign-regions, the operator structure. It should not depend on where or when you are. 后天 is the calibration — everything that does depend on locale, climate, institutions, century. And a calibration layer must therefore carry a stamp:

Plate 11 · 后天 must carry a stamp
H(locale, t) => H(Singapore, 2026) != H(Chang'an, antiquity)
How to read itA calibration layer without a place and a date is a contradiction in terms. The classical arrangement encodes a four-phase temperate year; at 1.35° north there is no such cycle, and an honest tropical calibration changes the frequency of the wheel rather than its values. A tropical 易 should care more about the hour than the season.
先天 invariant geometry后天 situated calibrationH the calibration itself

Mathematically the simplest honest version is a target: a point in the space that the state is pulled toward, with a strength, and — crucially — with a motion of its own. The situation is not a backdrop. It is going somewhere too.

Plate 12 · the whole system
xddot = sum_i a_i F_i(x, xdot) + h·( f(t) - x ) - c·xdot
How to read itEverything assembled: the mixture of operators, the pull toward a moving situated target, and a friction term so nothing runs away. Set the pull to zero and you have a person with no context. Hold the mixture fixed and freeze the target, and the whole thing comes to rest — which is Figure 12.
h the strength of contextf(t) the target, movingc friction

That result was not designed in. It appeared when the last of the arbitrary clocks was removed from an earlier version of the simulation, and it is the sort of thing worth keeping even though it makes the instrument less lively by default: a still life needs no explanation, and a moving one does.

i

The field moves

Institutions shift, monsoons turn, markets re-price, a city densifies. The target you are pulled toward is on its own clock, and whether it is coming toward you or moving away is a computable quantity — which decides whether patience is a strategy or a mistake.

ii

The mixture turns over

The 生/克 circulation from Chapter Nine. Even in a frozen situation, what you are doing cycles: storing gives way to branching, branching to amplifying, and around. This is the only motion in the model that requires no external clock at all.

End of part threeWhat is now assembled

Everything the instrument needs.

Six continuous lines in two cubes, sixty-four sign-regions. Velocities, so 变爻 has a countdown and arriving is distinguishable from leaving. Six clocks, so the stacking order does work. Five operators supplying the velocities, coupled so the mixture circulates rather than settles. And a moving, situated target with a strength.

That is a complete dynamical system, and it can be run. Part Four runs it — and reports what came out, including three findings that contradicted this project's own predictions and one arithmetic error that took four versions to catch.

Both defects found here have since been fixed in the Part Four instrument: 金 is smoothed, and the per-line clocks now scale the whole flow rather than the acceleration. Both were found by writing the explanation rather than by running the model, which is the strongest argument for why this sequence of documents exists.

The honest caveat to carry across: every coefficient in this part is asserted. The five operators were written to be qualitatively distinguishable — inward, outward, coupling, poles, threshold-commitment — and nothing above depends on their exact values. What the next part tests is whether the structure produces anything worth knowing, and the answer turns out to be yes in a way this project did not anticipate.
