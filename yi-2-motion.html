Where we areOne paragraph of recap

A snapshot, however rich, is still a snapshot.

Six lines became six numbers between −1 and +1. The sign of each is the classical 阴 or 阳, so nothing was lost, and the sixty-four sign-regions are still exactly sixty-four. What was gained is room: a trigram is an eighth of a cube, not a corner of one, and two situations sharing a glyph can sit far apart inside it.

But a point sitting in a region tells you no more about what happens next than a photograph does. The classical system knew this and patched it with a special mark — 变爻, the changing line — plus a great deal of commentary about what a changing line means. That patch is the thing we can now replace with arithmetic.

One warning about this part, because it matters for reading it honestly: nothing here explains why the state moves. This is kinematics only. We hand a point a velocity and watch. What supplies the velocity — 五行, read as a field of forces over the whole space — is Part Three. Keeping those separate is deliberate: it is the only way to see which insights come from motion itself and which come from a particular theory of what causes it.

Chapter fourA line with a velocity

变爻 bianyao becomes a number of time units.

Take the single dial from Part One and give it a rate of change. Two numbers now: where the line sits, and how fast it is travelling. Everything follows from dividing one by the other.

If a line is at x and moving at ẋ, then the time until it reaches zero — until the glyph flips — is simply tau = -x/xdot:

Plate 04 · the countdown
tau = - x / xdot valid when tau > 0
How to read itPosition divided by velocity, with a minus sign. When the result is positive the line is heading for its threshold and this is how long that takes. When it is negative there is no crossing ahead at all — the line is moving away.
x where the line sitsẋ how fast it movesτ time to the threshold

That is the whole of it. One division, and the tradition's most freighted concept becomes an ordinary reading on an instrument.

There is a second thing hiding in the sign of ẋ, and it is arguably the larger gain.

The glyph cannot tell you whether you are arriving or leaving.

A hexagram drawn at the moment a situation is consolidating and the same hexagram drawn as it comes apart are the same six marks. With velocities they are opposites: one has its lines moving away from their thresholds, the other has them heading in. Same name, opposite prognosis — and the difference is a sign, not an interpretation.

Plate 05 · arriving or leaving
sign(x · xdot) = -1 arriving at the threshold ; +1 leaving it
How to read itOne product, one sign, and the whole difference between a situation tightening and a situation coming apart. The classical notation has no place to record it, which is why the commentaries had to carry it in prose.
negative — converging on a changepositive — moving away from one

Chapter fiveThree lines, moving

A reading is a path, not a card.

Put the moving point back in the cube. It drifts; it crosses walls; the trigram name changes when it does. Nothing else about it changes at that moment — the crossing is an event in the notation, not in the situation.

Which reframes what a divination result actually is. Not a card drawn from sixty-four, but a label attached to the segment of a path you happen to be on when you look.

The drift here is deliberately stupid — constant velocity with a little friction, and a soft wall so the point cannot escape the cube. No forces, no theory, no 五行. Even so, two results are already visible, and both survive into the real instrument:

1

Readings have a shelf life

If your path crosses a wall in two time units, a name assigned now is worthless in three. The reading should carry that number. Part Four computes it properly, by running many slightly-different futures and asking when they stop agreeing.

2

Sixty-four are not equally likely

Some regions are transited in a blink, others dwell. Once there are real forces, this becomes measurable: a given situation visits some of the sixty-four constantly, some rarely, and some never at all. That is the 8×8 grid from Part One, shaded in.

The hexagram was never the situation. It was the name of the room the situation was passing through.
Chapter sixSix lines, six speeds

The stacking order is a set of clocks.

Here is the conjecture of this part — the one thing in it that is a genuine proposal rather than a consequence.

Classically the six positions of a hexagram are not interchangeable. Lower lines read as foundation, inner condition, what is beneath; upper lines as manifestation, outcome, excess, what is visible. Every commentary tradition treats a change in the bottom line differently from a change in the top. But the notation gives no mechanism for that difference — it is carried entirely by convention and by centuries of accumulated reading practice.

Suppose the positions are ordered by speed.

Assign each line its own time constant: the bottom line sluggish and structural, the top line quick and twitchy, the rest graded between. Formally, give line i a constant τᵢ, with tau_1 > tau_2 > ... > tau_6. Nothing else changes.

Plate 06 · six clocks
tau_i · xddot_i = F_i tau_1 > tau_2 > ... > tau_6
How to read itThe same force applied to every line, divided by a constant that differs per position. The ordering is the conjecture; the six numbers are a guess and are not fitted to anything.
τ₁ = 1.00 the base, sluggishτ₆ = 0.32 the top, twitchy爻位 position as timescale
Plate 07 · the error worth naming
xddot = F/tau (a change of mass — spread 1.1x) vs xdot = v/tau, vdot = F/tau (a change of clock — spread 3.2x)
How to read itDividing only the acceleration raises the natural frequency and the damping ratio together, and the two effects almost exactly cancel. The left-hand form looks like a time constant and behaves like a mass. Measured spread between line 1 and line 6: 1.1× against the 3.2× it should be.
left — what was built first, and what the instrument still doesright — an actual clock

One technical detail that turns out to matter a great deal. τ has to scale the whole flow — both how the velocity responds and how fast the position follows from it. Dividing only the acceleration by τ is a change of mass, not of clock: it raises the line's natural frequency and its damping together, and the two effects almost exactly cancel. This figure was built that way first and the six curves came out nearly identical — a spread of 1.1× where there should have been 3.2×. Worth recording, because the instrument in Part Four has that same bug, which means its per-line clocks have been much weaker than advertised.

Two diagnostics fall straight out, and they are the reason the conjecture is worth making:

↓

A crossing low down is a regime change

A slow structural variable that crosses zero will not cross back soon — its own sluggishness prevents it. Everything above must re-equilibrate around the new sign. This is exactly how the tradition reads a moving bottom line, now with a mechanism instead of a metaphor.

↑

A crossing at the top may be nothing

A fast variable sitting near zero flickers. Its sign is the least informative bit in the whole figure, and a "change" there may reverse before you finish reading it. The classical reading of the top line as excess or ending is, in this frame, simply the line with the worst signal-to-noise.

And it explains something the classical system asserts without accounting for: why the same hexagram means different things depending on which line is moving. If the six lines were equal-weight bits, that would be arbitrary. If they are measurements at six different frequencies, it is inevitable.

Where this stays honest: the ordering is testable and the numbers are not yet fitted to anything. Fitting them to one real domain — six observables with measurable relaxation times — is the most valuable unfinished job in the whole project, and it is the only one that requires leaving the model to do.

End of part twoWhat is still missing

Nothing here says why anything moves.

Every velocity in this part was set by hand. That is fine for showing what motion buys — countdowns, arrival versus departure, unequal dwell times, clocks for the six positions — but it is not a model of anything. A model has to say where the motion comes from.

Which is precisely the job the other half of the classical system was doing all along. 八卦 and 六十四卦 describe configuration: where a thing stands. 五行 describes transformation: how it moves. For centuries the mismatch between five and eight looked like an embarrassment to be reconciled. It stops looking like one the moment you notice they were never counting the same kind of object.

Eight is a count of positions. Five is a count of motions.

Part Three takes the five phases as five characteristic directions of change — storing, branching, amplifying, integrating, consolidating — and lets a mixture of them supply the velocities that were hand-set here. That turns the cube from a diagram into a field. It also brings in the second thing that moves a state, which no amount of internal dynamics can supply: the situation you happen to be in, and the fact that it is moving too.
